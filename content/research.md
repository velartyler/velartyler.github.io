## Starting Points

A few things that are true of speech perception:

* Speech is social. When we speak, we convey a great deal of information about ourselves alongside linguistic messages
* When we hear speech, we interpret and construct social meanings from the variation in the signal
* And speech perception isn't just auditory! We're integrating visual information about talkers and their vocal tracts
* But even across modalities, perception isn't just signal-driven. Listeners use their beliefs,  expectations, and knowledge of the world to inform perception

So, in any instance of anyone hearing any word at all, there is a manifold of information to be processed. I'm interested in how all that information (visual, acoustic, social, linguistic, and so on) gets integrated in perception such that we can arrive at the meanings we make out of it, and do so with surprising consistency.
